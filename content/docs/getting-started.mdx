## What STOIC needs, and why

STOIC asks for each permission only when you turn on the feature that needs it. Everything stays on your phone: there is no account and no STOIC server.

- **Usage access**: lets STOIC see how long each app was open. Needed for **My screen time**, **daily limits** and your **day score**.
- **Accessibility ("STOIC limits and pauses")**: lets STOIC see **which app is in front**, so it can show a pause, limit, bedtime or focus screen. It cannot read what is on your screen or what you type.
- **Notification access**: needed only for **Notifications** (counting and holding notifications).
- **Do Not Disturb access**: needed only if you want **Bedtime** to switch Do Not Disturb on.
- **VPN**: needed only for **Websites and internet**. The VPN stays on your phone.
- **Notifications**: so STOIC can tell you when a focus session ends or send your notification summary.

## Set up in five minutes

1. Open STOIC and go to the **Today** tab. The **Set up** card lists what is on and what is not.
2. Tap **My screen time** and then **Open Usage access settings**. Find STOIC and switch on **Permit usage access**. Come back.
3. Tap **Limits and pauses** and then **Open Accessibility settings**. Under **Downloaded apps** (or **Installed apps**), tap **STOIC limits and pauses**, switch it on and confirm.
4. Add the one or two apps that distract you most, and pick a pause or a daily limit.
5. Optional: set up **Bedtime** under **Controls**, then **Schedules and bedtime**.

<Note>

Start small. One app with a gentle pause works better than ten apps with strict limits you will want to switch off by Wednesday.

</Note>

<IfItDidntWork>

- **Android says "Restricted setting"**: open **Settings**, **Apps**, **STOIC**, tap the three dots at the top right and choose **Allow restricted settings**. Then try again. Android does this for apps installed from a file.
- **The switch keeps turning itself off**: see **If STOIC stops working**.

</IfItDidntWork>
